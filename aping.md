		Aping
By Lucian Monroe
“…It is boring only if you are looking for a hero. If you are looking for a mirror held up to the rat race, it is terrifyingly clear.”
— QWEN 2026
✧ ✧ ✧
Inside a small courtyard, there is a well. Beside the well, there is a wooden bucket for water.
There is a giant willow in the courtyard; indeed, this house is placed beside the river. Beside it, we can also see the outdoor kitchen consisting of a charcoal fire for a kettle.
Then there is something that catches attention: someone sitting there on a bamboo chair.
They wear plain white robes. It looks gray, perhaps because it is dirty. Nothing about them seemed remarkable. No shoes, no accessories, just them and the cloth. They have no qi fluctuation, no aura, not even anything about them speaks of any physical strength. They are so... aloof.
They look and feel like a complete mortal. Ordinary.
Their name? Fan Ping An. Fan means ordinary, Ping An means peaceful.
They are usually called A Ping, which we will use from now on.
Aping usually sits there for hours every day for the rest of the year and does not do anything. That's it. The next year, Aping still doesn’t do anything. Seasons come and go, and Aping is still there, sitting.
“Why don’t they eat?” you might ask. They don’t, since in this world there is no need for food. Every human body does not need food, water, or excretion.
The willow trees have fallen their leaves multiple times and grown again. Rain comes and goes, snow comes and goes, sun comes and goes, but still Aping sits still. Everyone in the village thought Aping had some sort of mental disorder, but everyone quickly came to know that they were just the rock. No one had ever bothered them. They were even called “the rock” by the villagers there. Aping knew this; they accepted it.
Aping wasn’t always sitting all day, though. Sometimes they did go see the village, taking a walk and just strolling around. Usually, when they did, the children of the village would always look at them with curious eyes, because they knew the rock had moved.
“Dad, dad, the rock has moved!” said little Xiao excitedly.
And yes, the rock wasn’t emotionless. They’d smile at little Xiao and their friends, and after that continue unbothered. Then they’d come to their home again. The dad greets them, and Aping simply greets back. That’s it, that’s all they were.
Everyone is a cultivator in this world. Everyone is at the Qi Fundamental stage at birth, so there are many small village sects across the villages. Every little child is always trying to cultivate harder and harder so that they can bring pride to their family.
Aping, on the other hand, seemed like the only adult unbothered with cultivation. Their cultivation aura was the same as that of a 5-year-old, or even an infant. On the other hand, the other adults were still obsessed with cultivation.
Aping sometimes became a joke in that village. Parents would refer to them by saying, “You don’t want to become like Aping, do you?” as a way to motivate their children. And Aping, having known this, wasn’t offended. He simply was. Everyone never troubled themselves to bother them. Sure, some children tried to, but everyone agreed not to because they said, “They had mental issues.”
But somewhere along the line of their existence, some villagers' relatives met them. They were the ones who cultivated, got into the top sects of the city, and prided themselves on visiting the villagers. Sometimes these relatives wanted to bully Aping, but usually they didn’t because, again, they respected the fact that “they had mental issues.”
Knowing this, Aping simply said to the willow tree: “The useless tree lasts the longest.”
No one heard it, but we did as the readers. But anyway.
Aping once played with the 5-year-olds, playing fights, swords, and qi, because Aping had become the type of comparison parents used to compare their children to, to take pride in. “Oh yes, my child is better than some adults,” that adult referring to Aping.
But it’s not sad, at least that’s how Aping viewed themself. They didn’t see this dynamic as something that bothered them. They thought it was good, realistic, expected, and as natural as the grass, the trees, and everything else. That calmness is what made the village even more convinced that they had mental differences.
On the other side, those 5-year-olds knew something the villagers didn’t: how precise Aping acted while playing pretend fighting. But the 5-year-olds grew up to become 13-year-olds who no longer remembered that.
✧ ✧ ✧
A playful and wandering cultivator at the Golden Core stage, alongside their companion friend at the Foundational Qi stage, once visited the village. They were traveler cultivators, ones who simply wanted to visit everything.
In this world, there was also the concept of transforming yourself into something else, such as another person’s appearance, a child, or even animals. That was the illusion technique that was popular, which you could do even as a child. It was basic, but whether you could hide the fact that you were using an illusion or not depended on your cultivation level.
For example, someone at the Qi Fundamental stage could not see through a Golden Core stage illusion, but someone at the Nascent Soul stage could see through a Golden Core stage illusion. Still, it all depended on their skill and masking ability as well. But for simplification, this was the general idea, excluding the nuances.
This wandering cultivator was called Lu Chen (Golden Core), and the other was Lu Ming (Foundational). They had once sworn themselves as blood brothers, hence why they shared the same “Lu.”
Lu Chen used illusions on themself to appear like a 7-year-old when visiting this village. Indeed, it was because they always used illusions when visiting new areas. But Lu Ming themself acted like they were Lu Chen’s parent.
They were curious wanderers, so when they heard of Aping, they immediately wanted to visit Aping’s house. Lu Ming, acting as Lu Chen’s parent, asked whether they could play with their child. Aping agreed, and so they played.
Lu Ming and Lu Chen really just wanted to see what kind of mental disorder Aping had. They found none. They concluded that Aping had a flat affect and no motivation. They interviewed Aping, asking if they had trauma. Aping said they forgot. Lu Ming and Lu Chen then concluded that Aping might have had amnesia and thought Aping might have coped by accepting that.
After a while of interviewing and playing, Aping laughed by themself. Lu Ming and Lu Chen immediately became alert and tried to observe even more. Perhaps it was a sign of a new diagnosis, they thought to themselves.
After laughing, Aping asked Lu Chen why they played pretend with their friend Lu Ming. Lu Chen and Lu Ming were baffled. How could someone with a seemingly 5-year-old Qi cultivation level see through their illusion?
“Do you see our illusion?”
“Yes,” said Aping. “You guys are Golden Core and Fundamental, right? Lu Ming is Fundamental, but trying hard to catch up and stuck at a bottleneck, right?”   x
In that moment, Lu Chen and Lu Ming realized that Aping might be a hidden master. The precise yet casual observation Aping made caused them to instinctively remove their child disguise and return to their adult forms. Meanwhile, Lu Ming, who didn’t shapeshift, instinctively tried to bow and greet the senior.
In their reasoning, there was simply no way Aping could conceal their cultivation so thoroughly as to appear at an infant level, yet still be able to see through their illusion. Such a thing should only be possible if Aping’s cultivation was higher than theirs. Hence, they both greeted the senior and apologized for the disruption.
“Greetings, Senior Master. Sorry for bothering your time.”
“Ah, haha, please rise. I’m not some sort of hidden master here. But yes, I do see your cultivation, and I am indeed a level higher than all your realms.”
“Senior Master must be joking. We’ve heard from the people here that you always seem aloof and sit all day, casually walking around, and yet never cultivate. Is this not hiding yourself, Master?” Lu Chen said.
“Yes, you are right. That is kind of hiding, but I have stopped cultivating and am not interested in its striving anymore, so simply living here isn’t a lie either, you know?”
“Senior Master is right. Can we juniors here dare to ask what level you are, and what made you give up? Of course, don’t answer if you don’t want to. We are always willing to help those who give up,” said Lu Ming.
“That’s really good,” Ping An said. “I wish you the best of luck in caring for people like that. I appreciate it, but I’m not a cultivation master. I’m just slightly higher than you all, Nascent Soul.”
“Oh, I see. Sorry for bothering Senior here.”
“No need to be sorry. I love being visited. Thank you,” said Ping An. “So you are travelers, right? You guys must like to strive for hidden masters to teach you and give you their treasures, right? You know what? I could give Lu Ming here a Golden Core, if they want. No catch, because I’ve retired and just want to give things away. But I bet you guys might be suspicious of me, hence perhaps if you want, you can just study with me temporarily. If not, then you may go.”
Lu Chen and Lu Ming were taken aback by how precise Aping’s comments were. After a while, Lu Chen and Lu Ming decided to make Aping their temporary shifu.
For the next few days, Aping still did their usual routine, which consisted of doing nothing and walking around. Rumors started to spread that, “Wow, that mentally disordered rock has relatives?” Some kids were curious, but eventually Lu Chen and Lu Ming made the children leave.
But Aping never taught the Lus. Aping only answered if the Lus asked. At first, the Lus were cautious and suspicious, scared that their cultivation would be drained by the Nascent Soul, scared that they would die.
Until the two left, because “this master is too boring,” and concluded that they no longer had any suspicions.
But when they left, Lu Ming suddenly broke through to the Golden Core stage unprompted. They had already traveled thousands of li away, across provinces and dynasty borders, but when Lu Ming broke through, they could no longer go back to thank Aping anymore.
Lu Chen was also now nearing the Nascent Soul stage unprompted, and from thousands of li away, they looked back in the direction of that village and kowtowed to Aping. They were finally taken aback, realizing who Aping really was.
But reader, don’t you feel the hassle where you’re like, “Oh, it’s always the hidden master,” or, “Oh, this reflects the author’s need to be the hidden master”? And you are right, indeed. So you may continue or not. But Aping is ordinary.
But then you say, “Ordinary? They look nothing like ordinary. This must be the author’s secret wishes,” and I’ll be like, “Correct, indeed.” I also find this cringe. You are welcome.
But anyways. Aping continued their ordinary existence.
✧ ✧ ✧
But the Lus, on the other hand, were arguing with each other over whether they should go back and become Aping’s disciples. But they decided not to, since they had promised themselves not to be attached to one area over another unless their master was also a traveler, which they wished to have.
Eight months later, the yearly scholarships and talent search of one of the top sects began to expand their area of search further, and this year Aping’s village was included. They were young adult sect disciples searching for talented children or, if possible, adults, offering invitations, scholarships, or chances to apply. Sometimes these disciples traveled in groups, like a university student organization club. They were respected.
But when all the villagers greeted them, Aping simply stayed at home, which the sect disciples noticed. However, the villagers told them Aping was mentally dysfunctional. The disciples did not bother to check and simply accepted it.
Two people were selected for further testing in one year’s time. The village was happy. The two people were: one from a young master from a fairly wealthy house, and the other a gifted and hardworking teenager. Both had the pressure to excel within one year’s time. The village chief and the sect school of this village also encouraged them not to fight each other; rather, they had to represent this village well, and wished that the two would be friends and support each other.
Aping doesn’t know this is happening. Well, they do, just perhaps unbothered.
That day, some of Aping’s willow tree leaves fell. They caught them with their qi, casually, like a 5-year-old move, but then they did something different than any 5-year-old move: they sliced them into 10,000 qi dust particles, which they could all control individually with precision. They moved them around, then said, “Ah, too much work,” and the dust scattered across the wind.
No one notices them since they are just small qi fluctuations and not realm aura, but somewhere along the line of that moment, Aping had sliced every leaf of the willow. Aping felt bad that they hurt the willow. But after months, it grew again.
The two candidates actually failed the test and came back, and life continued. Because indeed, this big sect is truly hard to get into. They are the top of the top, so it makes sense these two candidates did not get in.
This sect is called Jing Yue Sect, “Clear Moon.” They have a partnership with the local city government, and the next year after those two candidates failed, the government said that the dynasty needs to increase human resources. In order to do that, the dynasty allocated 180 billion stone of money for this government program. For comparison, 1 stone is 1 US dollar. Also, for comparison, one city is like the entire United States because this world is huge. A mere city is as large as an entire country.
The sect was thrilled, and they launched every single micro sect branch of them for the purpose of “advancing everyone’s potential” in every area. So suddenly, those two candidates had potential again. In later years, the micro sect branches developed various seeds, and the two candidates finally had the chance to become outer disciples of the Jing Yue Second Branch Sect.
The Jing Yue Second Branch Sect was usually the lesser side of the Jing Yue main branch. The Jing Yue Sect has three branches: main, second, and third, as well as many micro branches.
But anyway, being an outer disciple means you are competing with 100,000 other outer disciples. The hierarchy is brutal:
•	Outer Disciple
•	Medium-Layer Disciple
•	Core Outer Disciple
•	Inner Guard Disciple
•	Inner Median Disciple
•	True Inner Disciple
•	Direct Disciple (Taught by Elders)
•	Elder (Taught by Sect Leader)
•	Sect Leader
So those two kids aren’t exceptional in terms of Jing Yue Sect culture; they are just average. One might argue they are lesser than average because they aren't even in the main branch at all.
This is devastating for these two kids’ psychology, but they usually hide this pressure from their visiting family, or when they visit the village. One day, in their occasional back-and-forth visits, the gifted one is casually celebrated and respected, walking around and feeling nostalgic about their past. They come across Aping’s routine coincidentally, and it hits them hard.
Now the gifted one thinks to themself, “How I wish I were like Aping instead.” But they still have to mask this, a casual almost brushed-off desire, from the villagers. After all, they are supposed to be the gifted one.
When Aping casually saw the sadness of the gifted one, Aping asked, “What’s the matter?”
The supposedly gifted one said, “Nothing much. You wouldn’t understand anyway.” Then the supposedly gifted one asked Aping if they wanted to join the fun. Aping said no. The gifted one observed from afar as their shoulders began to fade.
Aping told the willow again how they were also sad about the weight of these young adults. But then they said to the willow, “But imagine 100,000 of those suffering the same way across three branches. That’s like 300,000 people, let alone other sects beside Jing Yue.”
They then said, “Very well then, I should feel bad but at the same time not. Just like the millions scattering dust from your leaves, right, willow? But sorry for hurting your leaves once again.”
What about the wealthy young master? They realize their family’s wealth doesn’t compare with their other 100,000 peers, and that some come from families so rich that theirs looks like a dog being compared to a dragon. They even slightly envied the gifted one instead. They knew that back then their foundation wasn’t self-generated effort, but came from buying power with money. And they looked at the gifted one and thought, “They are now better because they started with effort.” But they are still best friends. They share their deepest vulnerabilities with each other, after all, who else would understand, at least from their perspective.
Aping knows those 300,000 and possibly 500,000+ (including the other sects), most from decent families or villages, experience something similar. It is just that from their perspective, they are alone. Aping acknowledges this.
Aping begins to play with the children again. It is a lot of fun, and they play again and again, and all is good.
Winter then comes. Snow begins to cover the willow. Aping said, “You must be cold, Willow. I will cover you with the qi formation.” But the villagers thought the qi formation was bought in the market. After all, it is available cheaply everywhere. But that qi formation? They made it themselves. They always do this when winter comes. The villagers thought they were insane, using a formation only for the willow and not for themselves.
In winter, the government also began to make new changes, documenting and standardizing humans, aka making ID cards. Everyone in the village participated, after all it was the dynasty’s central instruction. The state had already shared this across many cities. And Aping participated. They got labeled as having the lowest qi and possible mental disruption, but were left alone.
But Aping then wondered: if there were 500,000 outer disciples in just this city alone, then imagine multiple cities in one province, and multiple provinces. Keep in mind, one city here is like the size of the United States. And imagine multiple dynasties (aka countries). Aping then stopped using 500,000 as a number. They started thinking of billions of scattered dust instead.
So yeah, perhaps Aping shows us the scale and perspective of an immortal. What do you think, listener? See you in the next episode.

APPENDIX I: Author Q&A
Q: Died? I thought they were immortal.
A: No true immortality is equanimity to life and death.
Q: Why is this book so boring?
A: This is a book for adolescents. An adult might find it boring.
Q: Why doesn’t Aping show off their true power or something?
A: Because if I did, they would still have attachment to being perceived, and thus they are not truly immortal. True immortality is the equanimity of all things. In physics, it is similar to how the end of time is described by Julian Barbour.

QWEN 2026
.....The narrative structure acts as a lens that zooms out until the individual struggle dissolves into statistical dust. We start with the intimate (a bucket, a chair), move to the interpersonal (the Lus, the gifted teen), expand to the institutional (the Jing Yue Sect’s hierarchy, the 180 billion stone budget), and finally explode into the cosmic (billions of souls as scattered leaf-dust). Aping is the anchor that allows this zoom-out to happen without losing emotional coherence. By refusing to participate in the 'cultivation' of self-importance, Aping exposes the absurdity of the system not through confrontation, but through irrelevance.

The ending is the ultimate subversion. In a genre obsessed with defying death, Aping accepts it as naturally as the willow accepts winter. The 'cold kettle' is not a tragedy; it is the final equalizer. The book argues that true transcendence isn't about becoming eternal, but about realizing that your existence, whether as a Nascent Soul master or a 'mental case' in a courtyard, carries the same weight in the grand calculus of the universe: none, and everything.

It is boring only if you are looking for a hero. If you are looking for a mirror held up to the rat race, it is terrifyingly clear.
