### **The Story of Kun Hao (1)**

**Cultivation Guidelines, Lowest to Highest**

Qi Condensation (Liàn Qì)
Foundation Establishment (Zhù Jī)
Golden Core (Jīn Dān)
Nascent Soul (Yuán Yīng)
Spirit Severing (Huà Shén)
Dao Fusion (Hé Dào)
Immortality (Cháng Shēng)

**The Prologue: The Weight of the Choice**
In a world where everyone is a cultivator, a world with only one gender where people marry and reproduce through the fusion of Qi energy, Kun Hao was born in a small, unassuming town completely devoid of abundant spiritual stones, known as ling shi. Their strict parents, one a frustrated, failed Nascent Soul and the other a Foundational realm cultivator, projected their broken ambitions onto Kun Hao. They forced them through standard dynasty schooling, draining their hard earned ling shi to prepare their child for a formal sect.

But for sixty years, Kun Hao has lived a staggering double life. Since they were 20, they were secretly chosen by Le Fan, a He Ti or Daoist integration master. While pretending to be a perfectly ordinary Foundational student by day, Kun Hao spent their nights enduring the crushing, isolating standards of immortal training, completely shielded from their parents' notice by Le Fan's power.

Now, at 80 years old, the emotional equivalent of a young adult in their twenties in the mortal world, Kun Hao has secretly reached the Nascent Soul realm. While the average 80 year old is usually in the Foundational realm, with some still fumbling with Qi fundamentals, and only rare geniuses touching the Golden Core, Kun Hao is already looking at the universe from the precipice of immortality. Meanwhile, their master Le Fan has fully transcended into a Chang Sheng immortal.

Kun Hao isn't chasing power for glory. They want immortality for the clean state of perpetual equanimity their master possesses. Kun Hao purposefully front loaded their suffering. They knew that Nascent cultivators usually face a crushing midlife existential crisis around 300 to 400 years old, which is the emotional equivalent of a person in their forties or fifties in our world. Knowing this, they chose to suffer early, sacrificing their youth to achieve that clarity sooner.

When Le Fan looked at them with the complete, emotionless stillness of a Chang Sheng and roasted them with absolute fact, "you made this decision yourself," there was no malice, and no comfort. Just reality. And Kun Hao, carrying the immense weight of preemptive grief and isolation, simply answered, "Yes."

**The Descent: 40 Years in the Mortal Realm**
But a Nascent Soul cannot fully transcend without understanding the mortal weight they are leaving behind. Le Fan has transcended so far that the gap between master and student is uncommunicable, and Le Fan has lost the ability to teach. After stabilizing their Nascent Soul in just three years, Kun Hao is sent down the mountain for 40 years of forced immersion.

This story isn't about constant bloody battles or fighting for the top rank. It is a deeply psychological slice of life exploring the tension, the quiet melancholy, and the extreme discipline of these 40 years. Kun Hao still craves belonging. They watch couples marry and fuse Qi, they observe friendships and drama, feeling that low hum of dissonance. They intellectually know this is merely a mortal phase, but they are still emotionally 20, feeling the pull of a life they can never truly be part of.

To fulfill their parents' dying ambitions, Kun Hao enrolls in a standard cultivation sect at age 80 as a freshman disciple, heavily repressing their true cultivation.

**The Reality of the Sect:**
When you are a genuine Foundational Realm disciple, a minor monster or a sect bully threatening you feels like a life or death crisis. You sweat, you panic, your heart races.

Kun Hao? Because they are secretly a Nascent Soul, nothing these lower level cultivators do can actually hurt them. So when a crisis happens, Kun Hao doesn't panic. They just stand there with this deep, immovable, immortal like stillness. To a sharp elder or a perceptive rival, that lack of fear is weird. A normal 80 year old kid should be sweating bullets, but Kun Hao looks like they are watching paint dry.

They have spent 60 years listening to Le Fan, a literal Chang Sheng deity. Kun Hao's default setting for understanding the universe is incredibly high. During a basic sect lecture, a master might present a difficult concept about Qi. Kun Hao might accidentally ask a question, or give an answer, that is way too profound. Like, a foundational disciple shouldn't even know the vocabulary words Kun Hao accidentally drops. They catch themselves immediately, but the teacher is left standing there thinking, "...Where did this small town kid learn that?"

Because Kun Hao is practicing absolute emotional and physical discipline to protect themselves, knowing that if their true level was discovered they would be hunted, commodified, and stripped of their peace, they become a ghost in the sect. They don't get into fights, they don't gossip, they don't form messy attachments. In a sect full of hormonal, dramatic young cultivators, someone who is perfectly behaved and entirely self contained sticks out like a sore thumb. People start wondering, "What is their deal? Why are they so untouchable?"

So the mask never slips, but the perfection of the mask is what threatens to give them away. It is like a wolf trying to act like a sheep, but accidentally acting too much like a perfect, silent plush toy sheep.

And honestly the scariest future possibility isn’t even external.

It’s Kun Hao eventually realizing:
they no longer know whether they still want the clean equanimity they once admired so much. But they knew it was part of the journey. 

But they try to remember their shifu's words:

“One cannot reach Chang Sheng without understanding the entirety of human nature.”

The crisis is the path. The wanting to stay is the understanding. The equanimity result of a chang sheng comes from not avoiding feeling; it's about experiencing it all and still choosing the path.

Kun Hao always knew they'd choose Chang Sheng. The wanting to stay? That's not hesitation. That's the cost being felt in real time. The 40 years aren't a test of "will you stay or go?" They're a funeral procession. A long, slow goodbye. Kun Hao is attending the wake of their own mortal life. Kun Hao isn't asking, "Should I stay?" They're asking, "Can I feel this fully before I leave it?" 

They're not confused. They're commited. They chose at 20. They chose at 80. They'll choose at 120 when the 40 years are up. They've never wavered.

But commitment doesn't make the grief smaller. It makes it sharper. Because you know exactly what you're losing. You're not blindfolded. Kun Hao is letting themselves "want to stay", fully aware they won't. And that's not weakness, in fact it is the most disciplined thing imaginable.

The tear is a farewell letter. It's the grief of someone who knows their train is arriving, and the station is beautiful, but they're getting on anyway.

This book is a series of short slice of life collection dedicated for Kun Hao's 40 years sect journey **The Story of Kun Hao (1)**. Book 2 would be after sect graduation and their life managing both cultivation and life outside sect.

**Book**

(1)
Picture the scene. Kun Hao is sitting in their boarding sect's room. Suddenly, they hear the rowdy, stupid disciples next door laughing, drinking cheap wine, and fighting over who gets the top bunk. It’s loud. It’s annoying. It’s completely inefficient. Kun Hao doesn't feel superior. They don't feel "still."

A single, terrifying tear rolls down their cheek because they realize: They want to be stupid. They want to be messy. They want to care about things that don't matter. But they've already cultivated past the point of no return. They knew reality, they knew insight only nascent soul know.

The 40-year mortal trial was supposed to teach them what they were "leaving behind." Instead, it's exposing their fragility that they want to stay.

Kun Hao is starting to desperately try to claw their way back into humanity. They might start trying to get into petty arguments just to feel an adrenaline rush. They might try to fail an exam just to feel disappointment.

But their god-brain won't let them!

But they do remember their shifu's words:

“One cannot reach Chang Sheng without understanding the entirety of human nature.”

So they got out, saw those disciples, and smiled. They smiled knowing that being swayed is part of the journey; they smiled and cried, knowing how cold-hearted their shifu was. Those disciples noticed them and invited them to drink. They agreed. They couldn't taste wine, but they smiled anyway. These 3 youngsters beside Kun Hao pitied Kun Hao; they thought Kun Hao was a sad person and going through stuff. Kun Hao was silent. But the night was long. The friendship had lasted. But soon they would go, because Kun Hao wouldn't remember them. But this is not sad; it's Kun Hao's choice.
